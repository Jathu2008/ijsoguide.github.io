Simple Pendulum Experiment

← Back to Mechanics Experiments

Experiment Overview

This experiment determines the acceleration due to gravity by studying the periodic motion of a simple pendulum. By measuring the time period for different lengths, the value of \( g \) can be experimentally calculated.

What This Experiment Demonstrates

  • Simple harmonic motion for small angular displacements
  • Relationship between pendulum length and time period
  • Experimental determination of gravitational acceleration

Apparatus Required

  • Light inextensible string
  • Metal bob
  • Retort stand and clamp
  • Meter ruler
  • Stopwatch

Theory

For a simple pendulum oscillating with small amplitude, the time period \( T \) is given by:

\[ T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{L}{g}} \]

Squaring the equation:

\[ T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2 L}{g} \]

A graph of \( T^2 \) against \( L \) gives a straight line, whose gradient can be used to calculate the value of \( g \).

Method

  1. Attach the bob to the string and suspend it from a fixed support.
  2. Measure the effective length of the pendulum from the point of suspension to the center of the bob.
  3. Displace the bob slightly and release it without pushing.
  4. Measure the time for 20 oscillations and calculate the time period.
  5. Repeat for different lengths.

Observations

Record the length of the pendulum and the corresponding time period. A table may be used to organize the readings.

Calculations

  • Calculate the time period \( T \) for each length.
  • Find \( T^2 \).
  • Plot a graph of \( T^2 \) vs \( L \).
  • Use the slope to calculate \( g \).

To learn more about how to plot graphs, visit our Graph guide page.

Result

The experimentally determined value of acceleration due to gravity is close to \( 9.8\, \text{m s}^{-2} \), within experimental uncertainty.
