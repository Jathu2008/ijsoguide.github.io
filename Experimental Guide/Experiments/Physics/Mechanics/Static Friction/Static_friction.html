Coefficient of Static Friction (Inclined Plane Method)

← Back to Mechanics Experiments

Experiment Overview

This experiment determines the coefficient of friction between a block and an inclined plane by finding the angle at which the block just begins to slide. At this critical angle, the frictional force reaches its maximum value.

What This Experiment Demonstrates

  • Existence of limiting friction
  • Relationship between friction and normal reaction
  • Determination of coefficient of friction using geometry

Apparatus Required

  • Adjustable inclined plane
  • Wooden block (or block with known surface)
  • Protractor or angle scale
  • Ruler or meter scale
  • Spirit level (optional)

Theory

When a block rests on an inclined plane, its weight can be resolved into components parallel and perpendicular to the plane. As the angle of inclination increases, the component of weight parallel to the plane increases.


At the point where the block is just about to slide, the frictional force reaches its maximum value, known as the limiting friction.

\[F_\text{max} = \mu _s R\]

At the critical angle \( \theta \), the forces balance as follows:

\[mg \sin \theta = \mu _s mg \cos \theta\]

Rearranging gives:

\[\mu _s = \tan \theta\]

Method

  1. Place the block on the inclined plane.
  2. Gradually increase the angle of inclination.
  3. Observe the angle at which the block just begins to slide.
  4. Record this critical angle \( \theta \).
  5. Repeat the measurement several times and take the average.

Observations

Note the angle of inclination at which the block starts to slide. Multiple trials help reduce random error.

Calculations

  • Measure the critical angle \( \theta \).
  • Calculate the coefficient of static friction using \( \mu _s = \tan \theta \).

Result

The coefficient of friction between the block and the inclined plane is equal to the tangent of the critical angle. This confirms the theoretical relationship \( \mu _s = \tan \theta \).
