Hooke's Law Experiment (Using a Spring)

← Back to Mechanics Experiments

Experiment Overview

This experiment investigates Hooke’s Law by studying how the extension of a spring varies with the applied force. By adding known masses and measuring the corresponding extension, the linear relationship between force and extension can be verified.

What This Experiment Demonstrates

  • Direct proportionality between force and extension
  • Validity of Hooke’s Law within the elastic limit
  • Determination of the spring constant

Apparatus Required

  • Helical spring
  • Retort stand and clamp
  • Mass hanger and slotted masses
  • Meter ruler
  • Pointer (optional, for accurate readings)

Theory

Hooke’s Law states that the extension of a spring is directly proportional to the applied force, provided the elastic limit is not exceeded.

\[F = kx\]

Here, \( F \) is the applied force, \( x \) is the extension of the spring, and \( k \) is the spring constant. As masses are added, the force increases according to \( F = mg \), causing the spring to extend.


A graph of force \( F \) against extension \( x \) should produce a straight line through the origin. The gradient of this graph gives the value of the spring constant \( k \).

Method

  1. Clamp the spring vertically to a retort stand.
  2. Measure and record the natural length of the spring.
  3. Attach a mass hanger to the spring.
  4. Add a known mass and allow the spring to come to rest.
  5. Measure the new length of the spring and calculate the extension.
  6. Repeat for increasing masses, ensuring the elastic limit is not exceeded.

Observations

Record the applied mass, corresponding force, and measured extension of the spring. Tabulate the values clearly for each load.

Calculations

  • Calculate force using \( F = mg \).
  • Calculate extension using \( x = l - l_0 \).
  • Plot a graph of force \( F \) against extension \( x \).
  • Determine the spring constant from the gradient.

For help with plotting graphs and finding gradients, visit our Graph Guide.

Result

The force-extension graph is linear within the elastic limit, confirming Hooke’s Law. The spring constant is given by the gradient of the graph.
