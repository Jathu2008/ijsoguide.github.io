Newton's Second Law Experiment

← Back to Mechanics Experiments

Experiment Overview

This experiment verifies Newton’s Second Law by studying how acceleration changes when the mass of a system is varied while the applied force remains constant. By adding masses to a trolley pulled by a fixed force, the inverse relationship between mass and acceleration can be observed.

What This Experiment Demonstrates

  • Acceleration decreases as mass increases for a constant force
  • Inverse relationship between mass and acceleration
  • Experimental verification of \( F = ma \)

Apparatus Required

  • Dynamics trolley (low friction)
  • Set of slotted masses
  • Pulley and light string
  • Fixed hanging mass
  • Meter ruler
  • Stopwatch or motion sensor

Theory

Newton’s Second Law states that the net force \( F \) acting on an object is equal to the product of its mass \( m \) and acceleration \( a \):

\[ F = ma \]

In this experiment, the applied force is kept constant by using a fixed hanging mass. Additional masses are placed on the trolley, increasing the total mass of the system. Since the force remains constant, increasing mass causes the acceleration to decrease:

\[ a = \frac{F}{m} \]

A graph of acceleration \( a \) against \( \frac{1}{m} \) should give a straight line, confirming Newton’s Second Law.

Method

  1. Set up the trolley on a horizontal track connected to a fixed hanging mass using a string and pulley.
  2. Ensure the hanging mass remains constant throughout the experiment.
  3. Measure the total mass of the trolley.
  4. Release the trolley from rest and measure the time taken to travel a known distance.
  5. Add known masses to the trolley and repeat the measurement.
  6. Keep the applied force constant for all trials.

Observations

Record the total mass of the trolley and the corresponding acceleration. Tabulate the values clearly for each trial.

Calculations

  • Calculate the applied force using \( F = m_\text{hanging} g \).
  • Calculate acceleration using \( a = \frac{2s}{t^2} \).
  • Determine \( \frac{1}{m} \) for each total mass.
  • Plot a graph of \( a \) against \( \frac{1}{m} \).

For guidance on plotting graphs, visit our Graph Guide.

Result

The graph of acceleration against inverse mass is linear, confirming that acceleration is inversely proportional to mass when the applied force is constant. This verifies Newton’s Second Law experimentally.
