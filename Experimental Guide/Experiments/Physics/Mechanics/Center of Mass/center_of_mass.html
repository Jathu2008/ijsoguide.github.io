Centre of Mass of an Irregular Object

← Back to Mechanics Experiments

Experiment Overview

This experiment determines the position of the centre of mass of an irregularly shaped lamina using the principle of suspension. By hanging the object from different points and tracing vertical lines, the point of intersection reveals the centre of mass.

What This Experiment Demonstrates

  • The concept of centre of mass for extended objects
  • Equilibrium of a body under gravity
  • Practical application of gravitational force acting through the centre of mass

Apparatus Required

  • Irregular lamina (cardboard, metal sheet, or wood)
  • Plumb line (string with a small weight)
  • Retort stand or nail for suspension
  • Pin or hook
  • Pencil
  • Ruler

Theory

When an object is freely suspended from a point, it eventually comes to rest with its centre of mass vertically below the point of suspension. This occurs because the gravitational force acts through the centre of mass, producing no net torque in equilibrium.


The vertical line passing through the suspension point and the plumb line represents the line of action of the gravitational force. When the object is suspended from multiple points, each vertical line must pass through the same point: the centre of mass.


The intersection of these vertical lines gives the position of the centre of mass of the irregular object.

Method

  1. Make a small hole near the edge of the irregular lamina.
  2. Suspend the lamina from this hole using a pin or hook.
  3. Hang a plumb line from the same suspension point.
  4. Once the lamina comes to rest, trace the vertical line along the plumb line on the lamina.
  5. Repeat the procedure by suspending the lamina from a different hole.
  6. Draw the second vertical line.
  7. The point where the two lines intersect is the centre of mass.

Observations

Each suspension produces a straight vertical line on the lamina. The lines intersect at a single point, which remains consistent regardless of the suspension point used.

Calculations

  • No numerical calculations are required for this experiment.
  • The centre of mass is identified graphically as the intersection of vertical lines.

Result

The centre of mass of the irregular object is located at the intersection of the vertical lines drawn from different suspension points.
