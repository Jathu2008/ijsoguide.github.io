Coefficient of Kinetic Friction (Horizontal Plane Method)

← Back to Mechanics Experiments

Experiment Overview

This experiment determines the coefficient of kinetic friction between a block and a horizontal surface by measuring the force required to move the block at constant velocity while varying the normal reaction. A graphical method is used to obtain the coefficient of kinetic friction.

What This Experiment Demonstrates

  • Kinetic friction acting during uniform motion
  • Proportionality between kinetic friction and normal reaction
  • Graphical determination of coefficient of kinetic friction

Apparatus Required

  • Wooden block
  • Horizontal surface (table or track)
  • Spring balance or force sensor
  • Set of slotted masses
  • Mass hanger (optional)
  • Meter ruler

Theory

When a block slides over a horizontal surface with constant velocity, the net force acting on it is zero. Therefore, the applied pulling force is equal in magnitude to the kinetic frictional force.

\[ F_\text{applied} = F_k \]

The kinetic frictional force is given by:

\[ F_k = \mu_k R \]

On a horizontal surface, the normal reaction \( R \) is equal to the weight of the block and any additional masses placed on it:

\[ R = mg \]

Substituting:

\[ F_k = \mu_k R \]

This equation has the form \( y = mx \). A graph of kinetic frictional force \( F_k \) against normal reaction \( R \) gives a straight line whose gradient is the coefficient of kinetic friction \( \mu_k \).

Method

  1. Place the block on the horizontal surface and attach the spring balance.
  2. Pull the block gently and adjust the force so that it moves with constant velocity.
  3. Record the reading of the spring balance; this gives the kinetic frictional force.
  4. Add a known mass to the block to increase the normal reaction.
  5. Repeat the measurement for several different total masses.
  6. Ensure the block always moves at constant speed during measurements.

Observations

Record the total mass on the block and the corresponding force required to maintain constant velocity. Tabulate values of normal reaction \( R \) and kinetic frictional force \( F_k \).

Calculations

  • Calculate the normal reaction using \( R = mg \).
  • Take the spring balance reading as the kinetic frictional force \( F_k \).
  • Plot a graph of \( F_k \) (y-axis) against \( R \) (x-axis).
  • Determine the gradient of the best-fit line.

For guidance on plotting graphs and determining gradients accurately, visit our Graph Guide.

Result

The graph of kinetic frictional force against normal reaction is a straight line passing close to the origin. The gradient of this graph gives the coefficient of kinetic friction between the block and the surface.
