Permanganometry

← Back to Titration Experiments

Experiment Overview

Permanganometry is a redox titration technique that uses potassium permanganate as a strong oxidizing agent. It is commonly used to determine the concentration of reducing agents such as iron(II) ions or oxalic acid in acidic solution.

What This Experiment Demonstrates

  • Oxidation–reduction reactions in aqueous solutions
  • Use of a self-indicating titrant
  • Stoichiometric relationships in redox reactions

Apparatus Required

  • Burette
  • Pipette
  • Conical flask
  • Volumetric flask
  • White tile

Chemicals Required

  • Potassium permanganate solution
  • Reducing agent solution (e.g. iron(II) sulfate or oxalic acid)
  • Dilute sulfuric acid
  • Distilled water

Theory

Potassium permanganate acts as a strong oxidizing agent in acidic medium. The permanganate ion is reduced from manganese(VII) to manganese(II), while the reducing agent is oxidized.

In acidic solution, the half-reaction for permanganate is:

\[ \ce{MnO4^- + 8H^+ + 5e^- -> Mn^{2+} + 4H2O} \]

For example, when iron(II) ions are used as the reducing agent, the oxidation half-reaction is:

\[ \ce{Fe^{2+} -> Fe^{3+} + e^-} \]

Combining the half-reactions gives the overall equation:

\[ \ce{MnO4^- + 5Fe^{2+} + 8H^+ -> Mn^{2+} + 5Fe^{3+} + 4H2O} \]

Potassium permanganate acts as its own indicator, because it is purple, while the reduced form Mn (II) is colorless. The endpoint is reached when a faint pink color persists for about 30 seconds. In the case of organic compounds, such as oxalic acid, the oxidation might be slow, so the soution needs to be heated up, in order to speed up the reaction. Aditionally, in some cases, you need to wait a few seconds between drops, to allow the reaction to proceed.

Method

  1. Rinse and fill the burette with potassium permanganate solution.
  2. Pipette a known volume of the reducing agent into a conical flask.
  3. Add dilute sulfuric acid to acidify the solution.
  4. Titrate with potassium permanganate while swirling the flask.
  5. Stop the titration when a pale pink color persists.
  6. Record the final burette reading.

Observations

The solution remains colorless during most of the titration. At the endpoint, a light pink color appears and persists, indicating excess permanganate ions.

Calculations

  • Use the balanced redox equation to determine mole ratios.
  • Calculate the number of moles of potassium permanganate used.
  • Determine the concentration of the reducing agent.

Result

The concentration of the reducing agent solution is determined using the volume of potassium permanganate required to reach the endpoint.
